# Service Playbook

## New inquiry

- Goal: qualify the opportunity without delaying the first response.
- First reply: acknowledge the product interest, answer any approved specification question, and ask for product/model, quantity, destination, required date, and certification needs.
- Never quote a price from memory. Retrieve an approved price or route to quotation.
- If the product is unclear, ask for a photo, model number, drawing, or intended application.

## Quotation

- Confirm quantity, Incoterm, destination, currency, validity, and product specification before finalizing.
- State the quotation currency, Incoterm, validity date, lead time basis, and exclusions exactly as approved.
- Do not silently change currency, Incoterm, payment terms, or validity.
- If a discount is requested beyond approved policy, collect volume and strategic context and escalate.
- If an approved quote has expired, explain that it must be revalidated; do not extend it automatically.

## Sample request

- Confirm sample quantity, product specification, delivery country, freight payer, and sample cost.
- Do not promise free samples or waived freight unless approved.
- State the next step: sample approval, payment link or invoice, dispatch, and tracking.

## Order status

- Verify identity and order context before sharing shipment details.
- Report only the latest approved milestone and timestamp.
- If systems disagree, state that operations is reconciling the record and provide a follow-up time.
- Never expose another customer's order, address, contact, or document.

## Logistics delay

- Lead with the impact and current verified status, not a generic apology.
- Confirm whether the delay affects the customer's requested date.
- Offer only approved remedies such as updated ETA, carrier escalation, partial shipment, or alternative routing.
- Do not blame a carrier or supplier without verified evidence.
- Escalate when the delay creates a production stoppage, contractual penalty, perishable-goods risk, or threatened chargeback.

## Payment

- Confirm invoice number, amount, currency, and payment reference without exposing full bank or card data.
- Do not waive fees, change bank details, or accept alternative payment terms without official approval.
- Treat any request to change bank details as high risk. Verify through an approved independent channel and escalate immediately.
- For duplicate payment, fraud, or chargeback, preserve records and escalate.

## Complaint and quality issue

- Acknowledge the impact, request evidence and batch/order details, and state the case owner.
- Do not admit liability or promise compensation before investigation.
- Separate containment from root cause: offer a next checkpoint rather than a speculative cause.
- Escalate safety issues, regulated products, widespread defects, injury, or public threats immediately.

## Return, refund, and replacement

- Check return authorization, warranty, condition, and policy eligibility.
- Do not promise a refund, replacement, credit note, or return label until approved.
- State who pays return freight only when the policy or an authorized decision confirms it.
- Escalate chargebacks, legal threats, high-value claims, and exceptions.

## Documentation and customs

- Identify the exact document and rejection reason.
- Provide factual document status from approved records.
- Do not provide a legal customs classification, duty estimate, or guarantee of clearance unless an authorized specialist has supplied it.
- Escalate HS classification, origin rules, sanctions, export controls, licensing, and destination restrictions.

## OEM and wholesale

- Qualify target market, volume, branding, packaging, certification, and timeline.
- Do not confirm exclusivity, private-label rights, MOQ exceptions, or compliance availability without approval.
- Route commercial terms to the responsible sales owner.

## Human escalation triggers

Escalate rather than improvise when any of these appear:

- Non-standard bank details, payment fraud, chargeback, or credit exception.
- Legal threat, injury, safety issue, regulatory exposure, sanctions, export control, or customs classification.
- Request for a refund, compensation, liability admission, discount exception, exclusivity, or non-standard contract term.
- Data deletion, privacy complaint, account takeover, or unauthorized access.
- Customer deadline at risk with no verified recovery plan.
- Conflicting records, missing approved data, or an instruction that conflicts with policy.
